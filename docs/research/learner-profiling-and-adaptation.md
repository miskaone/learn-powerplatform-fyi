# Learner Profiling & Adaptive Instruction Brief — Mastery Gate

**Purpose.** Map the evidence base on learner profiling / learner modeling and adaptive instruction — knowledge tracing, cognitive diagnosis, adaptive-instruction meta-analyses, open learner models, affect detection, and LLM-era modeling — onto Mastery Gate's actual mechanisms, and name evidence-backed profiling/adaptation techniques the product does not yet use. Companion to [`learning-science-alignment.md`](./learning-science-alignment.md), which covers the instructional-technique side (retrieval practice, feedback, spacing, interleaving).

**Scope note.** Mastery Gate's "learner model" is deliberately minimal and deterministic: a localStorage ledger (`mastery-gate:v1`) holding timestamped attempt records, a per-misconception fire-count map (`misconceptionFires` over a ratified 17-misconception taxonomy), four rubric scores (Recall, Connections, Application, Transfer — 0–4 each, gate opens only when every dimension ≥3), capped coach notes, drill/exam/debrief state, and an optional learner name. The only "profiling" inputs are graded answers, answer-choice misconception keys, and an optional `confidence: 'low'|'high'` hint the agent attaches to `request_next_action`; routing is a fixed six-row precedence table (gate-pass → advance; repeated misconception → coach; first miss → hint; second miss → review; correct+low-confidence → go_deeper; else continue). There is no probabilistic student model, no latency feature, no averaging, and no backend.

## 2. Per-topic evidence and alignment

### 2.1 Knowledge tracing and cognitive diagnosis: BKT, DKT, DINA/IRT

**Evidence.** Corbett & Anderson's Bayesian Knowledge Tracing models each skill as a latent learned/unlearned bit, updated per practice opportunity with four parameters (initial knowledge p(L₀)≈0.1–0.3, per-opportunity learning ≈0.1–0.2, guess/slip ≈0.1–0.2), advancing learners when p(known) crosses a threshold (0.95 in their ACT Programming Tutor) ([Corbett & Anderson 1995, *UMUAI*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01099821)). It has anchored three decades of ITS practice, with known limits: no forgetting, a one-bit knowledge assumption, guess/slip confounded with learning when skills get few observations, and better fit when parameters are individualized (Pardos & Heffernan's individualization line). Deep Knowledge Tracing replaced the Bayesian update with an LSTM, raising predictive AUC to ~0.80–0.85 on ASSISTments-scale data ([Piech et al. 2015, NIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/5654-deep-knowledge-tracing); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.05908)) — but later work showed DKT's internal reconstructions can be inconsistent with its own predictions and its activations resist interpretation ([Yeung & Yeung 2018; INRIA analysis](https://inria.hal.science/hal-04180391/document)). Cognitive diagnosis models take the complementary, discrete route: DINA/G-DINA estimate binary mastery of named attributes from a Q-matrix mapping items to attributes, with IRT modeling continuous ability on graded item parameters ([Junker & Sijtsma 2001, *Psychometrika*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02294469); [de la Torre 2011, *Psychometrika*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11336-011-9207-7); [DINA tutorial](https://mc-stan.org/learn-stan/case-studies/dina_independent.html); [MC-variant](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5978611/)). Their cost is data: stable attribute estimates need many items per attribute, and Q-matrix misspecification is the classic failure mode.

**Alignment with Mastery Gate.** The engine is a hand-built, deterministic cognitive diagnosis model: the 17-misconception taxonomy is the Q-matrix (every distractor is schema-validated to carry a named misconception id, ISC-13), `misconceptionFires` is a count-based attribute-evidence accumulator, and the coach tool registers only after the same misconception fires twice (ISC-26) — a deliberate "evidence threshold before diagnosis" rule that mirrors why DINA needs repeated observations. It forgoes BKT's probabilistic smoothing for auditability: the gate is a filmable referee (same inputs, same verdict), and the ISA bans any averaged mastery number (ISC-15) — consistent with CDM's finding that a single continuous ability score is the wrong unit for remediation. DKT-style neural tracing is architecturally impossible by design (pure TypeScript, no network, deterministic — ISC-11/12), which is the correct trade for a governing model even though a neural tracer would predict answers better offline.

### 2.2 Does adaptation itself add value? Meta-analytic evidence

**Evidence.** The large ITS effect sizes come from *step-level* adaptation: VanLehn found step-based tutoring (feedback at each reasoning step, d≈0.76) statistically indistinguishable from human tutoring (≈0.79) and far above answer-based CAI (≈0.31) ([VanLehn 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369)); Kulik & Fletcher's meta-analysis of 50+ controlled evaluations put ITS at a median ≈0.66 SD over conventional instruction ([Kulik & Fletcher 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420)). But when "personalized/adaptive learning" is evaluated at the platform level, effects shrink an order of magnitude: Major et al.'s meta-analysis of technology-supported personalised learning in LMICs found g≈0.17 (math) and ≈0.16 (reading), with most studies moderate-quality and few isolating adaptivity as a factor ([Major et al. 2021, *BJET*](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13116); [open-access EdTech Hub version](https://edtechhub.org/report/the-effectiveness-of-technology-supported-personalised-learning-in-low-and-middle-income-countries-a-meta-analysis/)). du Plooy et al.'s higher-education meta-analysis found adaptive learning raised academic performance in only 59% of studies and engagement in 36% ([du Plooy et al. 2024, *Heliyon*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11544060/)); a recent *JECR* meta-analysis reports medium-to-large effects of AI-enabled adaptive learning against non-adaptive controls ([JECR 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/07356331241240459)), but such comparisons confound adaptivity with better feedback and practice design. The pattern across reviews: **the active ingredient is fine-grained, error-specific responsiveness — not the existence of a learner model** ([Sibley et al. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959475225000659) adds evidence that adaptive teaching's gains persist at delayed test).

**Alignment with Mastery Gate.** Mastery Gate sits on the strong end of this gradient by construction: routing responds at the level of the individual answer, per question (miss #1 vs #2), per misconception (fire count ≥2), and per rubric dimension — closer to VanLehn's step condition than to Major's platform condition. Equally important is the negative result: the literature warns against crediting "adaptivity" itself, which is why the product's adaptive claims should be framed as *mastery-gated sequencing + misconception-keyed feedback* (mechanisms with their own strong evidence, per the companion brief) rather than as "AI personalization." The untrusted-agent architecture is also a hedge against this literature's known confound: in most adaptive platforms nobody can verify the model's verdicts; here every profiling input the agent supplies is validated/clamped against the ledger before it can move routing.

### 2.3 Open learner models: showing learners their own model

**Evidence.** Bull & Kay's open learner model (OLM) program makes the machine's representation of the learner available to the learner — by inspection, or through negotiation where the learner can challenge the model's beliefs ([Bull & Kay 2010, *Advances in ITS*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-14363-2_15); [SMILI framework, Bull & Kay 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S156042922600418X); [negotiated OLMs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6302918/)). Empirical effects are moderate but consistent: OLM/skill-meter interfaces in the Cognitive Tutor geometry curriculum improved self-assessment accuracy, motivation, and learning outcomes — with the largest benefits for lower-prior-knowledge students — though skill meters alone can inflate confidence without improving calibration ([Long & Aleven 2017, *UMUAI*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11257-016-9186-6); [Kay 2022 review](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/163675/2/1-s2.0-S2666920X22000248-main.pdf)). The mechanism is metacognitive: an inspectable model invites planning, promotes accurate self-monitoring, and lets learners catch the system's errors (negotiated forms).

**Alignment with Mastery Gate.** Mastery Gate ships a *partial* open learner model and a *closed* one. Open to the learner: the hub's per-objective progress ("N of 34"), per-lesson completion ("N of 7"), the lesson-ending mastery profile with prescribed drill, and the planned four-dimension rubric panel (ISC-33) — an inspection-form OLM with no averaged score, matching Long & Aleven's finding that explicit mastery display supports self-regulation. Closed to the learner: the misconception ledger (`misconceptionFires`) — the single most diagnostic structure in the product — is agent-facing (`get_learner_state`) but never rendered to the learner as a "your wrong answers share this faulty mental model" view. The debrief (composed strictly from ledger-verified misconceptions, ISC-57) is the only learner-facing misuse-of-model surface. This is the cheapest high-evidence gap in the product (see Gaps).

### 2.4 Affect and motivation profiling: confusion, frustration, boredom

**Evidence.** D'Mello & Graesser's affect-dynamics work with AutoTutor established the canonical cycle during complex learning: engagement → (cognitive disequilibrium) → confusion →, if unresolved, frustration → boredom, with confusion epistemically *productive* when resolved ([D'Mello & Graesser 2012, *Learning and Instruction*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2012.01.002)). Baker et al. found boredom the most persistent and most harmful state, associated with poorer learning and "gaming the system," while frustration was less damaging ([Baker, D'Mello, Rodrigo & Graesser 2010, *IJHCS*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2009.09.001)); D'Mello's meta-analysis of affect incidence confirms the distribution shifts across learning environments (D'Mello 2013, *JEDM*). Detection accuracy is workable from dialogue and context in ITS (sensor-based detection is weaker off the lab bench), but **intervention** evidence — systems that detect affect and change tactics, improving measured outcomes — remains thin and mixed; the strongest practical prescription is indirect: engineer conditions (appropriate challenge, resolution of confusion) that keep learners in the engagement/confusion band rather than trying to read and treat emotions in real time.

**Alignment with Mastery Gate.** The product implicitly encodes this literature's policy without detecting anything. The routing table *preserves productive confusion*: a first miss routes to a hint (disequilibrium with a ladder out), not to the answer; unresolved repeated misconception escalates to the dedicated coach (frustration containment); `correct + low-confidence → go_deeper` addresses the fluency-without-confidence state that precedes bored disengagement. Exam Mode's tool revocation deliberately induces the exact evaluative pressure under which frustration must be tolerated. Nothing senses affect — no sentiment, no facial/behavioral channel — which the evidence mostly supports avoiding at this scope: a deterministic proxy (miss count, repeat reviews) is more robust than a noisy detector. The gap is one-directional: the engine can *contain* frustration but cannot *notice* it early; the only signal is the agent's two-bin `confidence` hint, which describes the learner's stated certainty about the answer, not their affective state.

### 2.5 LLM-era learner modeling (2023–2026): promise and documented risks

**Evidence.** Three findings define the LLM-era state of the art:

- **LLMs can model learners from conversation — with validation.** Scarlatos et al. show LLM prompting plus fine-tuning can extract knowledge components from tutor–student dialogue and perform knowledge tracing over it (LLMKT), beating prior dialogue-KT baselines ([Scarlatos et al. 2025, L@S](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3706468.3706501); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16490)); parallel work decodes student behavior from pretrained-LLM representations ([Stanford SCALE, next-token KT](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/next-token-knowledge-tracing-exploiting-pretrained-llm-representations-decode-student)). These results are benchmark-relative: LLM-inferred ability tracks measured performance imperfectly and needs ground-truthed calibration — nobody has shown an unaided LLM's mental model of a learner matching a psychometric one.
- **LLM grading is consistent but biased.** GPT-4 grading of student work achieves high internal consistency (ICC up to 0.94–0.99; [Hackl et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2023.1272229)) and matches human *mean* scores, yet compresses score distributions (risk-avoidance of extremes; [Lundgren 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16510)) and shows length bias ([Zubaer et al. 2025, *Sci Reports*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-21572-8)).
- **LLM tutors over-scaffold and grade sycophantically.** Left unaligned, LLM tutors default to giving away answers rather than scaffolding — the motivation for "pedagogical alignment" fine-tuning ([Sonkar et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05000), building on SOLAR) — and RLHF-trained models systematically bend their stated judgments toward the user's beliefs, even flipping correct answers under pushback ([Sharma et al. 2023, ICLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)). Bastani et al.'s math RCT (see companion brief) adds the outcome-level warning: unguarded access degrades performance once removed.

**Alignment with Mastery Gate.** This is the product's founding risk model, operationalized structurally. Every documented LLM-profiling failure has an architectural countermeasure: over-scaffolding → the agent structurally cannot see answers (redacted schemas), so "give the answer" is not a reachable action; sycophantic grading → the agent does not hold the rubric, it *proposes* rubric scores that the engine clamps (0–4), requires verbatim evidence quotes for, excludes tool-emitted strings from the evidence corpus, and refuses entirely before the first graded attempt — the 2026-08-27 blocker fix closed the exact "agent grades by vibes/self-award" hole the sycophancy literature predicts. LLMKT-style conversational profiling is in a sense what the visiting agent does implicitly — but Mastery Gate never lets that implicit model drive routing: the agent's only sanctioned profiling inputs are one graded answer at a time and a two-bin confidence hint, both validated against the ledger. The remaining exposure is honest: the confidence hint is uncalibrated agent self-report, and the literature suggests treating it as weak evidence — which the routing table does (it only ever *adds* instruction on `low`, never skips the gate).

### 2.6 Cold start, model drift, and privacy in a static, localStorage-only site

**Evidence.** Cold start — predicting a learner before they have practice data — is a recognized, only partially solved problem in knowledge tracing, addressed by prior-knowledge seeding, meta-learning over new students, and specialized architectures ([Baker et al., EDM 2021](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/EDM21_paper_126.pdf); [csKT](https://pykt.org/cskt); [MAML-KT 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00137)). Model drift is younger evidence: KT models' predictions degrade as cohorts and content shift across academic years, and systematic drift-detection for student models is only now being studied ([robustness-under-drift study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00704); [EDM 2025 doctoral consortium](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.doctoral-consortium-papers.255/index.html)). On privacy: student profiling data is regulated data. GDPR treats profiling with automated effects under Article 22 with data-minimization duties ([GDPR Art. 22](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-22-gdpr/)); FERPA in the US protects education records but is widely criticized as too narrow for edtech surveillance and profiling ([Zeide, Notre Dame](https://scholarship.law.nd.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=11346&context=nd_lrev); [US Dept of Ed Student Privacy](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/)).

**Alignment with Mastery Gate.** Cold start: the engine assumes zero prior knowledge and zero fires for every misconception — a deliberately conservative prior equivalent to p(L₀)=0, which costs nothing pedagogically because the first miss routes to a hint rather than a verdict. Drift: there is no time dimension in the model at all — fire counts persist at full weight forever, so a misconception overcome in week one and a live one in week four are indistinguishable; the ledger's attempt timestamps make a decay policy implementable without any new capture. Privacy: the static, no-backend architecture is the strongest available privacy posture — profile data never leaves the device, so there is no transfer to regulate, no breach surface, and no Article 22 exposure (no automated decision made by a *remote* system about the learner; the learner runs the model on their own machine). Residual risks are local and already engineered against: localStorage is plaintext and tamperable (field-by-field validation on load, memory fallback on degradation), and the ledger holds a learner name plus full attempt history — hence the storage caps on coach notes and the fact that a tampered/tossed ledger simply resets the game rather than leaking anything. The one live obligation is honesty of scope: gate state must never be presented as a certification record.

## 3. Gaps — profiling/adaptation techniques not yet used

1. **A learner-facing misconception map (full open learner model).** The single cheapest evidence-backed addition (Bull & Kay 2010; Long & Aleven 2017). The ledger already contains everything: render `misconceptionFires` + per-question resolution as a learner-visible "your model" panel ("fired twice: platform-limit misapplied to custom connectors"), mirroring what `get_learner_state` shows the agent. Long & Aleven's caveat applies: show *evidence* (which questions fired it), not bare badges, to avoid uncalibrated confidence. A negotiated form — letting the learner mark "I've got this now," which routes to a verification question rather than clearing the count — is the classic OLM extension and stays fully deterministic.
2. **Latency-based ability signals.** Attempt records already carry timestamps, so inter-attempt latency and hesitation-before-commit are derivable with zero new capture. Response time is a classic adjunct to correctness in ability estimation (IRT response-times; BKT extensions). Concrete shape: engine-only features — very fast + wrong = confidence mismatch (favor coach over hint); very slow + correct = effortful retrieval (support the `go_deeper` row the agent's confidence hint currently triggers alone). Do not let latency touch the gate: it is noisy across devices and sessions, and the ISA's filmable-determinism principle requires the gate to stay answer-driven.
3. **Expanding spaced-retrieval queue keyed to misconception history.** The companion brief proposes a 1d/3d/7d review queue over resolved questions; keyed to the misconception ledger it gets stronger: questions whose misconceptions fired re-enter the queue preferentially, and a misconception leaves the "live" set only after N consecutively-correct re-retrievals *across* questions (discriminating "memorized this item" from "repaired the mental model" — the repeated-fire rule already distinguishes these).
4. **Per-misconception decay / freshness.** The drift literature (arXiv 2511.00704) and BKT's no-forgetting limitation both bear on the ledger's time-blindness. Concrete shape: a decayed fire count (e.g., each correct answer on a same-misconception question halves the count; days-old fires weigh less in the ≥2 threshold) — a one-function engine change with unit-testable semantics, no backend, no new data.
5. **Calibrated confidence beyond two bins.** The agent's `'low'|'high'` hint is uncalibrated self-report from a sycophancy-prone model (Sharma 2023). Concrete shape: widen to per-dimension confidence with an engine-side reconciliation rule (agent-low + rubric-evidence-missing ⇒ treat as low regardless of the agent's claim), and log the hint in the ledger so the debrief can show calibration ("agent said high on questions you missed 40% of").
6. **Cold-start pretest.** A 3–5 question pretest per objective (prequestions double as a learning technique per the companion brief) would seed `misconceptionFires` before instruction, making the first real miss route by history rather than blank-slate — the standard cold-start remedy, implementable as ordinary engine-scoped questions with errors explicitly forgiven.
7. **Data control surface.** Given the ledger holds a learner name and full attempt history, a learner-facing "export / clear my data" control (JSON download of `mastery-gate:v1`, one-tap erase) would make the site's privacy posture legible and GDPR-flavored by construction — data-minimization and portability as features, at near-zero cost.

## 4. Caveats — thin evidence, small effects, confounds

- **Platform-level adaptivity evidence is weak; step-level is strong — don't blur them.** Major et al.'s g≈0.16–0.17 and du Plooy's 59%-of-studies picture describe coarse personalization; VanLehn's 0.76 describes per-step tutoring. Claiming the latter's effect sizes for a product because it is "adaptive" would be the exact confound these meta-analyses warn about; what Mastery Gate actually does is mastery gating + error-keyed feedback (each with independent support), not continuous learner modeling.
- **Prediction accuracy is not instructional benefit.** DKT's AUC gains (and LLMKT's dialogue-KT gains) are about *forecasting answers*, not improving learning; no evidence shows a better-predicting model teaches better. The product's refusal to chase predictive accuracy is defensible, but so is the observation that its discrete counts discard signal BKT would exploit (partial credit, latency, error independence).
- **The misconception ledger's validity is assumed, not measured.** DINA's classic failure mode is Q-matrix misspecification; Mastery Gate's 17-misconception taxonomy was ratified by author review, never validated against learner data (no correction data showing that a named distractor's misconception predicts future errors on that misconception). The two-fires coach threshold is an engineering choice, not an estimated parameter.
- **OLM evidence is moderate and learner-dependent** — gains concentrate in lower-prior-knowledge learners, and uninformative skill meters can inflate confidence without calibration gains (Long & Aleven). A learner-facing misconception map could backfire if it reads as surveillance or a permanent record of failure; framing matters and is untested here.
- **Affect intervention evidence is the thinnest band in this brief.** Affect *dynamics* are well-replicated; *detected-then-treated* pipelines improving outcomes are not. Nothing in Mastery Gate should claim affect adaptivity — it engineers conditions, it does not detect states.
- **LLM-modeling results are benchmark-era and predate tool-mediated agents.** LLMKT and grading studies evaluate generic chat corpora and essay grading, not a WebMCP agent operating under schema redaction with engine-clamped inputs; sycophancy results are model- and version-dependent. The product's defenses are prudent extrapolations, not empirically validated against this exact interface.
- **Privacy posture is strong but not absolute.** No transfer means GDPR/FERPA barely bind a static site, but localStorage is readable by any script on the origin, survives clearing expectations users don't have, and a learner name plus attempt history is personal data even at rest; the honest claim is "data never leaves your browser," not "anonymous."
